Authorisation workflow

What we changed

The main changes in the draft designs, page by page.

Updated 20 November 2026

Across the service

  • Built only from GOV.UK Design System components, so it is quick to build and accessible.
  • Pages lead with the thing the user came for: the claim on a claim page, the agreement on an agreement page.
  • Every action is recorded in the history: what changed, from what to what, who did it, their role at the time, why, and the date and time.
  • Dates are written in full, or as 5/11/2026 in tables where space is tight.

Claims

Claim details

  • The claim comes first. The agreement is a short summary alongside, and the full history is below. No tabs.
  • The claim number is the heading, with its status as a tag. A banner says what the claim needs next.
  • Action buttons depend on the claim’s status.
  • Only details this service can change have a "Change" link, so it is clear what can be changed.
  • The claim history shows who did each thing and their role at the time, with a status column for changes waiting for approval.
{{ screenshot(draftPath + "/claim-details", "Claim details") }}

Changing claim details

  • Change several details, check them on one page, then save once. Based on the GOV.UK check answers pattern.
  • One reason covers all the changes, and they are one entry in the history.
  • Changes that affect eligibility, such as the test result, go to a team lead for approval first.
{{ screenshot(draftPath + "/claim-edit/check", "Check your changes, after changing the vet’s details and the test result") }}

Add a note

  • Add a note to any claim from the claim history.
  • Notes cannot be changed or deleted, like the rest of the history.
{{ screenshot(draftPath + "/add-note", "Add a note") }}

Withdraw claim

  • A withdrawal is a request. A team lead approves it before the claim is withdrawn, and the claim stays in check while it waits.
  • Uses the live fields, with an "Other" option for the reason and how the issue was found.
  • Shows the agreement holder, so the caseworker can check who they are speaking to.
  • Records who asked for the withdrawal and how they contacted the RPA.
  • Warns that a withdrawal cannot be undone and the farmer will no longer see the claim.
{{ screenshot(draftPath + "/withdraw-claim", "Withdraw claim") }} {{ screenshot(draftPath + "/claim-details", "Claim details, with a withdrawal request waiting for approval") }}

Claim withdrawn

  • No separate page. Once a team lead approves the request, the claim page shows the claim as withdrawn, with a banner saying when, who requested and approved it, and why.
{{ screenshot(draftPath + "/claim-withdrawn", "Claim withdrawn") }}

Agreements

Agreement details

  • Same heading pattern as the claim page: the agreement number as the heading.
  • New table of the agreement’s claims, each linking to the claim.
  • Address written in sentence case, one line per part.
{{ screenshot(draftPath + "/agreement-details", "Agreement details") }}

Change log

  • One place to see what has changed, on claims and agreements, and what is waiting for approval.
  • One row per change, showing who made it and their role at the time. When a team lead decides, the same row shows who approved or rejected it.
  • Says how many changes are waiting for approval, with a link to just those.
  • Filter by claims or agreements, type of change, scheme (IAHW or PBR), status and who made the change.
{{ screenshot(draftPath + "/change-log", "Change log") }}

Review a change

  • Team leads approve or reject a change from the change log, seeing who made it, why, and each detail from and to.
  • Requests to withdraw a claim are reviewed the same way, showing the reason, how the issue was found and who asked.
  • Rejecting needs a reason. Approving can have optional notes. Both are recorded in the history for the person who made the change.
  • An approved change updates the claim. A rejected one does not.
  • Team leads cannot approve their own changes.
{{ screenshot(draftPath + "/review-change", "Review a change, showing the reject notes") }}

Users (new)

  • Super admins can see everyone with access, their role and when they were last active, to support access reviews.
  • Each user has a role history, so the role shown in a claim history can be checked for that date.
  • Names in the claim history link to the user.

Not changed yet

  • The claims, agreements and flags lists are copies of the current pages.
