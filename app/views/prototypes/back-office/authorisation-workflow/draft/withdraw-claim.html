{% extends "prototypes/back-office/layouts/authorisation-workflow.html" %} {# Notes on design mock ==================== Authorisation workflow: withdraw claim (draft v3) The form a back office user fills in to ask for a claim to be withdrawn. Opened from the "Withdraw claim" button on draft/claim-details.html. A withdrawal needs a team lead's approval. Olayinka (BA) confirmed that, under the authorisation workflow, team leads and case officers can request a withdrawal, but only a team lead can approve or reject it. So "Send for approval" does not withdraw the claim. It goes back to the claim page, which shows the request waiting for approval, and adds a Pending row to the change log. A team lead approves or rejects it on draft/review-change. Once approved, the claim shows as withdrawn. The route is in app/views/prototypes/back-office/routes.js ("Request to withdraw the claim"). The warning also says the farmer will no longer see the claim in their account once it is withdrawn, as Olayinka confirmed, so the caseworker can tell the customer. Fields ------ Based on the live "Confirm withdrawal of this claim" screen: - Warning: "Once approved, a withdrawn claim cannot be undone." - Reason for withdrawal: the live reasons, with their hints. "Other", with a text box, is added by this draft. - How the issue was discovered: the live options. "Other", with a text box, is added by this draft. - Details on why the claim should be withdrawn: required text box. Olayinka confirmed these are the mandatory fields. The "Other" options and the contact fields below go beyond them. We are keeping them, to discuss: a farmer can ask for a withdrawal, but all 5 live reasons are data entry errors, so none fits that request. See ../notes.html. Added by this draft, to confirm in the design review (the live screen does not record the customer's request): - The agreement holder and business are shown at the top, so the caseworker can check the caller against the record. - Choosing "Customer contacted the RPA" reveals: the name of the person who contacted the RPA, the organisation they represent (optional, for example an agent or vet practice) and how they made contact. Three fields is the most a conditional reveal should hold. If research shows agents and vets often call, move these to their own page. The request is dated and timed when it is sent. Who is asking for the withdrawal is not asked. It is the signed-in user, shown on the page and recorded automatically, as GOV.UK guidance is not to ask for information the service already has. Built only with GOV.UK Frontend macros and classes. The "Other" text boxes use the radios component's conditional reveal, which is GOV.UK Frontend JavaScript, not bespoke code.
