Back office optimisation

Design notes

First thoughts on the prototype screens, with suggested changes, Olayinka’s answers to our questions, what we agreed on 20 November, and what we still need to agree.

Steve Molesworth
18 November 2026, updated 20 November 2026

The links on this page go to the draft designs. The designs before this work are in the poultry prototype’s back office.

Suggested changes

All pages

  1. Write dates in full in text and summary lists, as GOV.UK style advises, for example 5 November 2026. Where space is tight, such as the change date in the change log and dates in the claim history table, use the short form 5/11/2026, without leading zeros.

Agreement details

  • Draft design: agreement details
  1. Lead with the agreement number, not the business name, to match the claim page. Make the heading the agreement number alone (“IAHW-NQG3-7WZY”), with a small “Agreement” caption above it and the status underneath.
  2. Show the agreement details in this order:
    • agreement number
    • agreement type
    • agreement date
    • agreement holder
    • SBI number
    • address
    • business email
    • flagged
    • number of herds and flocks
  3. Write the address in sentence case, not capitals, with each part on its own line. Keep the postcode in capitals.
  4. Add a Claims for this agreement table in the lower half of the page. It would show:
    • the claim number, linking to the claim
    • the claim type
    • the date created
    • the date last updated
    • the current status

Claim details

  • Draft design: claim details
  1. Put the claim first. Remove the Claim and History tabs, and do not add a contents list. In the main two-thirds column, show:
    • the claim details at the top of the page
    • the action buttons
    Show the claim history as its own table below, running the full width of the page, as it will hold a lot of notes.
  2. Move the agreement information out of the main column. It belongs on the agreement page. For now, show a short summary in a one-third column to the right of the claim details, with the agreement number linking to the agreement. Later it could move underneath the claim, or be replaced by a single link to the agreement. To discuss with Olayinka.
  3. Make the heading the claim number alone (“FUBC-HJ9N-S5PK”), with a small “Claim” caption above it.
  4. Show the claim status as a tag after the claim number in the heading, and as plain text in the claim details, straight after the claim date. If a claim is withdrawn or pending review, show a notification banner beneath the claim heading that links down to the claim history, so users can see what was done and by whom. GOV.UK guidance puts notification banners above the page heading, so this placement is a deliberate choice to test.
  5. Show action buttons only to back office team members, for example to withdraw or revoke a claim. Which buttons appear depends on the user’s role and the claim’s status.

Claim withdrawn

  • Draft design: claim withdrawn
  • Draft withdraw claim form: claim withdrawn
  1. Remove the separate claim withdrawn page. Show a withdrawn claim on the claim details page instead, with its status at the top set to Withdrawn.
  2. Add a notification banner at the top of the claim page saying when the claim was withdrawn, by whom and why, with a link down to the claim history.
  3. Record the withdrawal in the claim history: the date, who withdrew it, the reason, how the issue was discovered, who made contact and how (if the customer contacted the RPA) and the details.
  4. Hide the action buttons once a claim is withdrawn.
  5. Add a Withdraw claim page, opened from the claim page, based on the live “Confirm withdrawal of this claim” fields. It keeps the warning “Once approved, a withdrawn claim cannot be undone” and asks:
    • the reason for withdrawal, using the live list of reasons and hints, plus a new “Other” option with a text box
    • how the issue was discovered, using the live options, plus a new “Other” option with a text box
    • if the customer contacted the RPA: the name of the person who made contact, the organisation they represent (optional, for example an agent or vet practice) and how they made contact (new, to confirm)
    • details on why the claim should be withdrawn
    The page shows the agreement holder and business at the top, so the caseworker can check who made contact against the record. It also shows the withdrawal date, set to the day the page was opened. It does not ask who is withdrawing the claim. The service records the signed-in back office user automatically and shows their name on the page.

Change log

  • Draft design: change log
  1. Keep the title Change log. The page will hold many types of change, as listed in the Type of change filter, not only changes to claims. Each row needs to show its type of change clearly. How best to support many types in one table is still to be worked out.
  2. Lead with the claim. Show the columns in this order: claim number, claim type, agreement number, agreement type, change, change date and status. Put the filters in the same order.
  3. Make Claim type its own column, for example Review or Follow-up, and add a filter for it. It currently sits under the claim number.
  4. Add an Agreement type column showing the abbreviation (IAHW or PBR). There’s already a filter for agreement type, but the table does not show it.
  5. Shorten the Status filter options to Pending, Approved, Rejected and All, without the word “changes”.
  6. Remove the Details column. The table is there to get users to a claim or an agreement, and the claim and agreement numbers already link to them.
  7. Keep the Type of change filter as it is.

Olayinka’s answers

Olayinka (BA) answered our questions on 20 November 2026. Most answers confirm what the draft already does.

Already in the draft

  • Withdrawal fields: the reason, how the issue was discovered and the details are mandatory. The draft uses the live fields.
  • Undoing a withdrawal: a withdrawn claim cannot be undone. The draft has no way to undo one.
  • Other actions: there is no revoke. Recommend to pay and Recommend to reject already exist and are not changed by the authorisation workflow.
  • Claim types: Review and Follow-up are the only ones.
  • Approval statuses: Pending, Approved and Rejected, as agreed in the design workshop with the RPA.
  • Types of change: URN, test results, date of visit, vet’s name, vet’s RCVS number, herd CPH and flags. The draft has all of these.
  • Agreement types: IAHW (livestock) and PBR (poultry) are the only ones.
  • Address format: sentence case, one part per line, will be recorded as a finding and built as an enhancement.

What we changed because of the answers

  • Withdrawal needs a team lead’s approval. Team leads and case officers can ask for a claim to be withdrawn, but only a team lead can approve or reject it. The withdraw claim form now sends a request for approval. The claim stays in check while it waits, and the request shows as Pending in the change log. The team lead reviews it like any other change. Once approved, the claim shows as withdrawn.
  • The farmer can no longer see a withdrawn claim. The withdraw form, the review page and the withdrawn claim say so, so the caseworker can tell the customer.
  • Times in the history. The claim history records the date, time, action and user, so the claim history, change log and review page now show the time as well as the date, for example 2:15pm.

Agreed with Olayinka on 20 November 2026

From a call between Steve and Olayinka, reviewing the latest designs.

  • 2 types of back office user: case officers (the RPA’s Recommender, Processor and User roles) and team leads (Super admin). The personas page now shows just these, with what each can do. Helen Marsh and Leila Haddad are now case officers.
  • Withdrawals: case officers and team leads can request one. Only a team lead can approve or reject it, and no one can approve their own request.
  • Telling the farmer: the farmer should get an email when their claim is withdrawn. Designed.
  • Telling team leads: today nothing tells a team lead that changes are waiting. They have to check the change log. A daily email will say how many changes are waiting and how many came in the last 24 hours, with links to the filtered change log. Designed, with a new “When” filter on the change log.
  • Telling the person whose request was rejected: Steve proposed they get an email with the team lead’s reason. We will not keep the rejected request for them to send again. Designed.
  • Who can change what: we will take a table of every detail on a claim and agreement to the RPA, saying whether it can be changed and whether a case officer can change it or must request it. A first pass is on the Who can change what page.
  • Flags: today only agreements can be flagged, by team leads, and a flag on an agreement covers everything the customer has. We will propose flagging claims too, and test it with the RPA. Designed (see below).

Flags: what we are proposing

  • Flag an agreement (the customer, a serious concern) or a claim (something found while checking it).
  • Anyone can flag, from the agreement or claim, as it changes nothing for the customer. Each flag has a type and an optional note, needed for “Other”.
  • The person who created a flag, or any team lead, can remove it, and must say why.
  • A claim shows when its agreement is flagged. An agreement shows how many of its claims are flagged. The claims and agreements lists show both, next to the SBI, with filters.
  • On the claim and agreement pages, flags show in the blue banner as warning text (with the exclamation mark). GOV.UK notification banners only come in blue and green, so we have not made it red.
  • The flags list no longer has “Create flag” or “Delete flag”, and has filters for what is flagged and who flagged it.

Still to agree

  1. “Other” options on the withdrawal form: a farmer can ask for a withdrawal, but all 5 live reasons are data entry errors. The draft keeps “Other”, with a text box.
  2. Recording who asked for a withdrawal: the draft asks for their name, organisation (optional) and how they made contact. Should the name and contact method be mandatory? Can agents or vets ask?
  3. Claim statuses: we still need the full list. Is “waiting for withdrawal approval” a status of its own?
  4. Selected for check, or being checked: about 1 in 4 claims is selected for checking. It would help to tell “selected for check” from “check in progress”, and perhaps to assign a check to a person. Parked for now, as it adds a lot.
  5. Flags: whether the RPA wants claim flags as well as agreement flags, the list of flag types, and whether a case officer’s flag needs approval (the RPA backlog says so).
  6. Which details need approval: see Who can change what.
  7. The daily email: when it is sent, whether it goes to every team lead, and whether they can turn it off.
  8. The farmer’s email: customer-facing wording for each withdrawal reason, and whether they can make a new claim for the same visit.
  9. What the claim history shows: the draft also shows the user’s role at the time, the approval status, notes and what changed from and to. We think this helps audit.
  10. Quick search on the claims and agreements lists, updating as people type: not answered yet. See the notes below.
  11. Fewer columns on the claims list: it has 8 columns (claim number and type, SBI, flags, species, herd or site name, CPH, claim date and status), which is a lot to scan. We suggest removing species and CPH.
    • Species can stay as a filter, and is on the claim page. The claim number also shows it, for example “BC” in REBC for beef cattle, though new staff may not know that.
    • CPH is mainly useful when checking one claim, so it can be on the claim page only.
    • Questions for Olayinka and the RPA: which columns do case officers use to pick a claim to work on, and do they sort or scan by species or CPH? Is herd or site name needed, or would the claim page do?

To discuss with the RPA team

Olayinka suggested we discuss these with the RPA team. The draft stays as it is until then.

  • Agreement information on the claim page: a summary in a column to the right (the current draft), a summary underneath the claim, or just a link to the agreement.
  • Agreement details: whether caseworkers need the agreement holder’s email and “Eligible for automated data redaction”. The draft leaves them out.

Quick search notes

Caseworkers often know the claim number, agreement number or SBI they want. Could the list update as they type, showing partial matches after a short pause (about half a second), with no need to select Search?

  • GOV.UK guidance advises against updating results as people type or change filters. This is mainly because screen reader users are not told the page has changed, and it can move focus. That matters most on public services. This is a back office tool used every day by trained staff, so there is room to do it differently.
  • If we do it, it must still meet accessibility rules (WCAG 2.2). Screen readers would need to hear the number of results as they change (a live region). The Search button should stay, so it still works without JavaScript.
  • It needs a search service that can return partial matches quickly, so check with the developers what the back end can support.
  • It needs custom JavaScript, so it cannot be shown in this prototype, which uses GOV.UK components only. It would be tested in research or a coded spike.
